## cercando-la-trincea::intro
A fairly hazy late-November day, but the temperatures aren't too low, so a trip to the valley is possible. We head off calmly, once again, toward Pezol. Unfortunately, when we arrive the route we'd planned to do is already taken by storm. No matter, we move to the nearby "Cercando la Trincea".

## cercando-la-trincea::outro
A simple, beautiful route. The continuity of the climb is often broken up by ledges and terraces where the belays sit. Along the pitches themselves, though, the climbing is more sustained. The route is well protected and easy to back up if you feel the in-place protection isn't enough.

## ciao-rita::intro
Mid-November is bringing freezing temperatures to Trentino, especially at night. But if it's sunny on the wall, and there's no wind, it's comfortable and you can climb without suffering too much from the cold. To be safe, we head for the Pezol wall, which gets sun all day, and despite -3°C at the parking lot we were reasonably comfortable. Today's route is "Ciao Rita".

## ciao-rita::outro
Despite its obvious lack of continuity, this route still has its appeal thanks to the excellent rock quality and pleasant slab sections. It remains a very simple, extremely well-protected line with great sun exposure for the winter months.

## attraversate::intro
Second outing in a row to Covolo di Butistone, chasing some morning cool relief on this first of May marked by lots of sun and, above all, lots of heat. Today we're the first to arrive, so we have plenty of choice on what to climb. We're tempted by the line "Attraversate" ("Crossings"), a name that says a lot about the type of route it is.

## attraversate::outro
A beautiful, interesting route that winds across the wall looking for its weakest points. The climbing is mostly technical and the bolting good, if a little spaced out in places. Especially worthwhile are the traverses along the middle pitches, which run over consistently solid, compact rock.

## eliseo::intro
The beautiful sunny day pushes us toward the walls of Valsugana, after attempting, without much success, a line on the third pillar of Martincelli the previous weekend. This time we head further south, specifically to Covolo di Butistone, intent on climbing the route "Eliseo".

## eliseo::outro
The last pitch is currently not passable as described. Vegetation has unfortunately swallowed up the whole upper part of the wall and the line is no longer visible. Either way, you can exit by following the ledge to the right beyond the belay until you reach the summit clearing, zig-zagging where the terrain allows. A route that definitely needs cleaning up, but overall offers interesting climbing. The middle pitches especially are beautiful, running over consistently solid rock in search of the wall's weakest points. The bolting is a bit dated but good, though spaced out and questionably placed in places. A good option for a half day if you're in the area.

## sofia::intro
The day promises stable weather only in the morning, so we opt for something short, just to put our hands on rock without having to wait another week. We head to Coste dell'Anglone, in the Piramide Lakshmi sector, to tackle a decidedly little-climbed line: Sofia.

## sofia::outro
A route that clearly suffers from few repeats. Overall very disjointed and forced; only a couple of pitches are really worth climbing. The protection is there, but you need to supplement it in many spots for safe progression. It remains an exploratory, mountaineering-style line — good training for more demanding routes.

## davide-pinamonti::intro
A gorgeous day across the whole region, with quite pleasant, at times even warm, temperatures. You can feel spring slowly arriving even though the peaks are still nicely white. Unfortunately we don't have much time today, but we still manage a quick hit-and-run in the Arco area. At the Mandrea pillars, the line "Davide Pinamonti" awaits us.

## davide-pinamonti::outro
The sixth and last pitch is the most demanding of the route and climbs a cracked slab up to the wide ledge where the line ends. From the belay you traverse a few metres right to reach the vertical line of bolts on the wall. You go straight up until you reach an obvious detached nose, where the line bends right. The steep, holdless slab above is climbed on aid, reaching the base of the crack that accompanies the climbing to the end. The climbing here isn't easy and often involves lie-backing with your feet on the sloping ramp at the base of the crack. There's no fixed protection now, so you need to place cams, which is always possible even if sometimes a bit awkward. The section isn't very long but it's far from trivial. The final blocky rock leads to the summit ledge and then to a tree belay. 32m, VI+ and A0. The route is currently quite neglected, with vegetation often blocking the way and hiding the protection. A real shame, because some pitches are interesting — the first, the second, and the last among others. The bolting is good but needs supplementing along the cracks, especially on the last pitch, which is demanding after the A0 section. Given how short it is, it's a good option for uncertain-weather days, or to combine with something nearby given the quick descent.

## luna-85::intro
It's shaping up to be a not-very-sunny but fairly warm Sunday. Looking for something long and good training ahead of the summer season — not so far off now — we eye a line on the Zebra-Striped Slabs wall that, despite the countless routes we've climbed in the valley, we haven't touched yet. We head for the start of "Luna 85", a classic of the wall.

## luna-85::outro
A route not to be underestimated. Even though the grades on paper are modest, the spaced-out bolting and the style of climbing demand full mastery of the grade and of pure slab technique. Overall it's a line that offers beautiful smearing passages where trusting your feet is often the only option. The commitment and engagement are high, and the satisfaction once at the top is huge — as is the fatigue, the tiredness, and the ache in your feet!

## archai::intro
A day that, according to the forecast, was supposed to be beautiful and sunny but turned out overcast and cold, not helped by an annoying little breeze. We're already down in the valley now, so all that's left is to suffer quietly and make the most of the morning. We head to Coste dell'Anglone intent on climbing "Archai".

## archai::outro
A worthwhile route, sustained in difficulty and well cleared of vegetation. The line winds about looking for the path of least resistance and elegantly overcomes the difficulties it meets. The protection is there, but you often need to supplement it, and where that's not possible you need to be able to move confidently through careful, deliberate climbing. The harder moves can be aided past, as noted in the topo, though they can comfortably be free-climbed too. The only shame is that in a few spots the holds are a bit polished by time.

## castagnarte::intro
A weekend with early-spring-like temperatures pushes us down to the valley to fully enjoy the sun warming the walls. We head to the ever-crowded Pezol wall, fairly confident our chosen route will be free given its unusual grade compared to the other lines on the wall, and the fact that little information about it can be found. ## castagnarte::outro
A route of three faces: the first two pitches are beautiful and worthwhile, if currently a bit dirty. The next three pitches, up to the wide ledge, lose interest as you go, while the last two pitches to the summit are too forced and add nothing to the climb. Still a decent workout, saved by the remarkable view over Lake Garda.

## luce-e-colori::intro
December 21st, the shortest day of the year. The sun shines high in the sky and the few hours of daylight need to be used to the fullest. ## luce-e-colori::outro
A beautiful, worthwhile line, sustained in difficulty and mountaineering in character, with protection often far apart that demands constant supplementing. After the somewhat physical lower pitches, the upper ones are won with delicate, balance-based moves. The central wall is particularly beautiful — colourful and made of solid conglomerate. ## la-piccola-piramide::intro
With the days finally starting to get longer, the range of routes you can climb in the valley in a single day is expanding too. We head to the busy Dro area, determined to climb the route "Archai", which unfortunately is already taken, so we fall back on the neighbouring "La Piccola Piramide" ("The Little Pyramid") right next to it. The peace of being first that day, and the dream of staying the only ones, are shattered by the noise of groups marching toward the wall. Time to climb.

## la-piccola-piramide::outro
At the end of the eleventh pitch, V+. The last pitch hosts the hardest moves of the whole route. It starts by skirting left around the belay niche along a continuous, overhanging little wall. The climbing is sustained on holds that aren't great and often far apart, requiring solid lock-offs and long reaches to get to them. Past the first slings you reach a ring, to the left of which, a bit lower down, there's a wide upside-down pocket where you can rest before the last move: pairing the hold with your right hand too, swapping fingers in and out one at a time, you raise your feet well onto the highest available holds, loading up and lunging for the obvious, distinctly good flakes above. You then continue straight up for a few more metres until the summit vegetation blocks the way and a rightward traverse begins along easy, compact blocky rock. This, without particular difficulty, leads to a low-angle ramp that eases off and reaches the top of the rock band, where you belay on a solid shrub. A properly sustained pitch demanding endurance and good power — definitely harder than the stated VI. 30m, VI/VI+. Overall a beautiful climb, not too hard but hosting interesting moves throughout. The rock quality is good the whole way, and the bolting is plentiful, though there's still room to place quickdraws on gear where needed. The variety of styles suits everyone: slabs, corners, chimneys, and small overhangs alternate for a fun, complete climb. Just watch out for traffic on the wall — avoid weekends if possible.

I set off with conviction, hoping to reach the sun and warmth a few metres higher, and very cautiously commit my weight to holds that feel, by touch, fairly solid, though none of them really give me a sense of security, and every so often a loose stone flies off below. Here you can quickly shake out the initial effort with a good rest before setting off again up a fairly well-featured corner. Soon I surmount this too, and a series of stepped ledges brings me to the first belay. (6b, 4a) The second pitch is a diagonal traverse under a roof. Here the rock becomes more solid and compact, and the sun creates a more ideal climbing temperature. Past the two initial ledges, you traverse right to a bolt placed under a visible hourglass feature. Here the line suggests a direct approach to the exit beside the roof, but holds are scarce and the difficulty rises considerably. Chalk marks from previous parties show the correct way: you traverse diagonally left on comfortable holds to the base of the roof. From here you traverse right to reach the exit. Key here is reading the easier route without letting yourself be swayed by the shorter one. A fantastic example of what it means to read the wall. The route continues with an exposed traverse on grey slab with your feet smearing. This traverse is notable for angling slightly downward, all the way to the second belay. (6a, 5c for the traverse).

## porci-con-le-ali::outro
The sixth and final effort of the route is on a gorgeous, super-compact slab. The roof isn't too high and has genuinely good holds, even though your feet dangle in space. Getting over it takes an athletic leap. Past this, 10 easy metres of slab lead quickly and without much difficulty to the end of the route. (5c, 5b) Overall the climb was rewarding, with some lovely climbing on very varied rock, from the jagged, loose red rock to the solid, sticky grey slabs. The techniques vary a lot on this route too, moving from vertical to horizontal with the traverses, from the explosive moves of the roofs to the delicacy of the slab. ## la-cengia-rossa::intro
After a long break due to less-than-kind weather, we finally got together on Friday evening to decide Sunday's route. Among the many candidate names, one stood out above the rest: "La Cengia Rossa" ("The Red Ledge") on the San Paolo wall at the foot of Monte Colt, a 7-pitch route between 5a and 6a. On Sunday we meet at 13:15, ready for the short trip to Arco, which took 45 minutes, calm and without excessive traffic. The sun accompanied us the whole way there, raising our hopes for a beautiful day. Arriving at our destination, we park just past the trail that zig-zags up toward the San Paolo crag, and set off toward the start of the route. Unfortunately the sun is already leaving us, since the wall faces fully west, but we don't let that discourage us — helmet, harness, carabiners, slings, and off we go. The route starts unpretentiously on steps for about 20 metres on solid, grippy rock, to get a bit of a feel for the route. The monotony of the stairway is broken at the end of the pitch by a small pillar jutting boldly out from the wall. The logic of the route suggests the best way past it: climb it centrally, then traverse right to reach the belay ring. To gain height and reach the comfortable scoop up and to the right of the block, on one side of the pillar, hidden, there's a good flake for the left hand. Here shoulder work is essential: you raise your feet, lock off, and reach with your right hand. Once at more comfortable holds, an elegant traverse takes you to the belay. (4c, one move of 6a)

## cima-alle-coste-parete-di-sherwood-via-little-john::intro
