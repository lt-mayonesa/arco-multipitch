## cercando-la-trincea::intro
A fairly hazy late-November day, but the temperatures aren't too low, so a trip to the valley is possible. We head off calmly, once again, toward Pezol. Unfortunately, when we arrive the route we'd planned to do is already taken by storm. No matter, we move to the nearby "Cercando la Trincea".

## cercando-la-trincea::outro
A simple, beautiful route. The continuity of the climb is often broken up by ledges and terraces where the belays sit. Along the pitches themselves, though, the climbing is more sustained. The route is well protected and easy to back up if you feel the in-place protection isn't enough.

## ciao-rita::intro
Mid-November is bringing freezing temperatures to Trentino, especially at night. But if it's sunny on the wall, and there's no wind, it's comfortable and you can climb without suffering too much from the cold. To be safe, we head for the Pezol wall, which gets sun all day, and despite -3°C at the parking lot we were reasonably comfortable. Today's route is "Ciao Rita".

## ciao-rita::outro
Despite its obvious lack of continuity, this route still has its appeal thanks to the excellent rock quality and pleasant slab sections. It remains a very simple, extremely well-protected line with great sun exposure for the winter months.

## eliseo::intro
The beautiful sunny day pushes us toward the walls of Valsugana, after attempting, without much success, a line on the third pillar of Martincelli the previous weekend. This time we head further south, specifically to Covolo di Butistone, intent on climbing the route "Eliseo".

## eliseo::outro
The last pitch is currently not passable as described. Vegetation has unfortunately swallowed up the whole upper part of the wall and the line is no longer visible. Either way, you can exit by following the ledge to the right beyond the belay until you reach the summit clearing, zig-zagging where the terrain allows. A route that definitely needs cleaning up, but overall offers interesting climbing. The middle pitches especially are beautiful, running over consistently solid rock in search of the wall's weakest points. The bolting is a bit dated but good, though spaced out and questionably placed in places. A good option for a half day if you're in the area.

## davide-pinamonti::intro
A gorgeous day across the whole region, with quite pleasant, at times even warm, temperatures. You can feel spring slowly arriving even though the peaks are still nicely white. Unfortunately we don't have much time today, but we still manage a quick hit-and-run in the Arco area. At the Mandrea pillars, the line "Davide Pinamonti" awaits us.

## davide-pinamonti::outro
The route is currently quite neglected, with vegetation often blocking the way and hiding the protection. A real shame, because some pitches are interesting — the first, the second, and the last among others. The bolting is good but needs supplementing along the cracks, especially on the last pitch, which is demanding after the A0 section. Given how short it is, it's a good option for uncertain-weather days, or to combine with something nearby given the quick descent.

## luna-85::intro
It's shaping up to be a not-very-sunny but fairly warm Sunday. Looking for something long and good training ahead of the summer season — not so far off now — we eye a line on the Zebra-Striped Slabs wall that, despite the countless routes we've climbed in the valley, we haven't touched yet. We head for the start of "Luna 85", a classic of the wall.

## luna-85::outro
A route not to be underestimated. Even though the grades on paper are modest, the spaced-out bolting and the style of climbing demand full mastery of the grade and of pure slab technique. Overall it's a line that offers beautiful smearing passages where trusting your feet is often the only option. The commitment and engagement are high, and the satisfaction once at the top is huge — as is the fatigue, the tiredness, and the ache in your feet!

## archai::intro
A day that, according to the forecast, was supposed to be beautiful and sunny but turned out overcast and cold, not helped by an annoying little breeze. We're already down in the valley now, so all that's left is to suffer quietly and make the most of the morning. We head to Coste dell'Anglone intent on climbing "Archai".

## archai::outro
A worthwhile route, sustained in difficulty and well cleared of vegetation. The line winds about looking for the path of least resistance and elegantly overcomes the difficulties it meets. The protection is there, but you often need to supplement it, and where that's not possible you need to be able to move confidently through careful, deliberate climbing. The harder moves can be aided past, as noted in the topo, though they can comfortably be free-climbed too. The only shame is that in a few spots the holds are a bit polished by time.

## castagnarte::intro
A weekend with early-spring-like temperatures pushes us down to the valley to fully enjoy the sun warming the walls. We head to the ever-crowded Pezol wall, fairly confident our chosen route will be free given its unusual grade compared to the other lines on the wall, and the fact that little information about it can be found. Still a decent workout, saved by the remarkable view over Lake Garda.

## pirata-samu::intro
After the pair of short, easy routes the day before, we move a few kilometres north to tackle something a bit longer and more demanding. We head toward Brentino-Belluno Veronese and walk up to the Spalti di Capitel d'Orsa wall, which looks out sideways onto the "Santuario Madonna della Corona". Today's route? Pirata Samu.

## pirata-samu::outro
A route that would definitely benefit from a restyle and a thorough cleaning. Where the wall is clear of vegetation the rock is lovely and the climbing genuinely enjoyable. Overall, with a good clean-up, "Pirata Samu" is a nice route in a quiet, peaceful area — sanctuary bells permitting!

## via-del-dottore::intro
We head down into Valle del Sarca toward Dro, intending to tackle a route at Coste dell'Anglone early in the morning. ## lungo-il-fiume-e-sullacqua::intro
The walls around the village of Tessari definitely offer plenty of options for easy, short climbs. Not just the ease of the routes, but the equally convenient and quick approaches and descents, open up possibilities even for the laziest climbers. In recent years these walls have indeed been besieged in every sense: both in terms of foot traffic and the constant stream of new routes being added, painting the base of the wall with red route names every couple of metres. The route we're reviewing here, though, isn't a recent addition. It has, however, apparently recently been re-equipped in terms of protection and is listed as S1 in modern printed guidebooks. At the base of the wall where the route climbs, the full name should be present, but the only thing you can make out is a white rectangle. Above it, as if scratched with fingernails, you can just about spot some reference to the word "FIUME" ("RIVER"), but only the last 3 letters are clearly legible. From the start you can just make out the first 2 bolts, at about 8-9 metres off the ground. Here we start to doubt whether we're on the right line, but the topo image, showing the little roof right above the start to be skirted on the right, keeps us from hesitating too long, and we head off decisively into this new adventure.

## lungo-il-fiume-e-sullacqua::outro
The third pitch climbs angling well to the right for the next 2 protections, spaced slightly apart. Past 2 trees above us, a beautiful vertical slab appears in front of us. As beautiful as it is unprotected. We study the wall carefully but find no piton, bolt, or sling indicating that's the way to go. Looking left instead, we notice, almost by accident among the trees, a bolt and the next belay. A shame, because the slab looked genuinely beautiful and not too hard. The route instead forces us into a hollow, fighting with vegetation once again. Our green spirit calms other kinds of spirits. Here too the topo grades this pitch 3c over 20 metres, but more realistically it's a 4c over 15 metres. The fourth and fifth pitches, if they can even be called that, offer little satisfaction: just a low-angle slab at the start of the fourth pitch. The line then plunges into the little wood where a few tracks and a path head toward the wall opposite. Moving left, and entering among some trees, you can also spot red lettering from another route, or perhaps the "Pensieri" variant. 3c, 20m, 2a, 20m. The wall opposite has beautiful worked, pocketed rock, very well featured and rough, offering easy but still fun climbing. The first protection here too sits at about 10 metres up, the next one 7 metres further, and the last another 7 metres after that. At the exit of the route there's a bolt with a ring. The correct grade for this pitch is around 5a, even though the guidebook lists it as 3a. 30 metres. Overall the route is nice, without great pretensions. Some route-finding choices are certainly debatable, but we honestly don't understand how it could have been rated S1 for protection and have so many grading errors. Especially for such easy routes, more care in compiling the reviews is essential. People taking their first steps in this sport, tempted by the ease of the route and the close-together protection, could indeed find themselves in unpleasant situations along the climb. In general we don't recommend repeating it, except to adventure lovers.

## porci-con-le-ali::intro
Today, first time on a rope with Umberto. When it came to choosing the route we were quite torn among the thousand options Valle del Sarca offers, but partly for the fairly modest difficulty, the length, and above all the name, we settle on "Porci con le Ali" ("Pigs with Wings") on the San Paolo wall. We meet at 8:45 in Trento, so we're on the wall around 10:00-10:30. The morning is very cold, mist everywhere, and a real struggle to de-ice the car windows, but off we go anyway — pigs, but tough ones. Arriving at the base of the very long San Paolo wall, it takes us a while to find the start of the route, hidden at the bottom of a gully not very visible from the trail running along the wall. The sun finally peeks out, and a warning awaits us: The route has two distinct starts. The first, the original, follows an easy, tree-lined ramp of grade IV. The second, decidedly more demanding and rewarding, climbs straight up red rock, not always solid, for two pitches until joining the original, with difficulties of 6b and 6a respectively. We decide to go with the latter option to make the climb more interesting.

## porci-con-le-ali::outro
Overall the climb was rewarding, with some lovely climbing on very varied rock, from the jagged, loose red rock to the solid, sticky grey slabs. The techniques vary a lot on this route too, moving from vertical to horizontal with the traverses, from the explosive moves of the roofs to the delicacy of the slab. "The pigs were there; luckily the wings weren't needed."
